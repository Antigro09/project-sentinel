### A. Problem Target

**Outcome:** The surviving candidate is **Boundary Contract Dual Ledger (BCDL)**. It is mathematically coherent under restrictive assumptions, but its integrated novelty and AGI usefulness remain **UNKNOWN**.

- **Capability gap:** Long-horizon systems conflate two questions:

  1. Which intervention changed the outcome?
  2. Which part of the world model contradicts verified evidence?

  BPTT transports derivatives; return decomposition transports scalar reward. Neither inherently supplies a compositional semantic repair certificate.

- **Formal task family:** A finite acyclic typed causal program with modules

\[
M_i=(I_i,O_i,R_i,\{K_i^\iota\}),
\]

where

\[
R_i\subseteq X_{I_i}\times X_{O_i},\qquad
K_i^\iota:X_{I_i}\rightsquigarrow X_{O_i},
\]

and persistent state must be represented explicitly at module boundaries.

Let \(A\) be an intervention variable, \(a\) an intervention, \(a_0\) a reference intervention, \(Y\) an outcome, \(u:Y\to[-U,U]\), and

\[
G\subseteq X_0\times X_H
\]

a verified end-to-end behavioral contract.

The proposed mechanism maps conditionally to distribution-shift transfer, compositional reasoning, continual refinement, causal modeling, and macro-level planning.

### B. New Mathematical Construct

Two candidates were examined.

**Candidate 1: Counterfactual Cutset Dividends — REJECTED.**

Define

\[
V(S)=\mathbb E[u(Y)\mid do(a_S,a_{0,\bar S})]
\]

and Möbius interaction dividends

\[
\delta(T)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T|-|S|}V(S).
\]

This reduces directly to a Harsanyi/Shapley-family attribution. [Counterfactual Shapley Credit Assignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16999) already applies this construction to temporal causal credit. Recursive culprit sets also collide with model-based diagnosis. Renaming coalitions as “causal cutsets” would not create a new formalism.

**Candidate 2: Boundary Contract Dual Ledger — RETAINED AS HYPOTHESIS.**

BCDL maintains two noninterchangeable mathematical ledgers.

**1. Causal-effect ledger**

A boundary \(C\) is interventionally sufficient when an action-invariant downstream kernel \(Q_{C\to Y}\) satisfies

\[
P_a^Y=P_a^C Q_{C\to Y}
\]

for every admissible intervention.

Define

\[
\zeta_C^{a:a_0}=P_a^C-P_{a_0}^C,\qquad
v_C=Q_{C\to Y}u,
\]

and

\[
\mathcal L_C(C;a,a_0,u)
 =(\zeta_C^{a:a_0},v_C),\qquad
\kappa_C=\langle\zeta_C^{a:a_0},v_C\rangle.
\]

The stored object is the signed boundary measure \(\zeta_C\), not merely the scalar \(\kappa_C\).

**2. Epistemic-revision ledger**

For relations \(P:X\to B\), \(S:C\to Y\), and \(G:X\to Y\), define residuals

\[
(P\backslash G)(b,y)
\iff
\forall x\,[P(x,b)\Rightarrow G(x,y)],
\]

\[
(G/S)(x,c)
\iff
\forall y\,[S(c,y)\Rightarrow G(x,y)].
\]

For module \(R_i:B\to C\), prefix \(P_i\), and suffix \(S_i\),

\[
A_i^\star=P_i\backslash(G/S_i)
\]

is its greatest globally safe local contract. Therefore

\[
D_i=R_i\setminus A_i^\star,\qquad
R_i^{\mathrm{safe}}=R_i\cap A_i^\star.
\]

If \(E_i\subseteq R_i\) is protected positive evidence, revision is admissible only when

\[
E_i\cap D_i=\varnothing.
\]

An identified update is

\[
\mathcal H_i\leftarrow
\{h\in\mathcal H_i:
E_i\subseteq R(h)\subseteq A_i^\star\}.
\]

Intuition:

- Causal influence is transported as an interventional boundary difference, not a derivative.
- Utility covectors move backward while signed causal measures move forward.
- World-model correction is a certified relational meet, not a gradient step.
- Failed reward alone cannot revise the dynamics model.
- Multiple valid diagnoses remain explicit until an intervention distinguishes them.
- Typed macro-contracts permit substitution without exposing internal implementation.

### C. Theoretical Results

**Theorem 1 — Cut-invariant causal-credit conservation**

**Status: PROVEN on paper; mechanical verification UNKNOWN.**

Let \(C,D\) be successive sufficient cuts. Suppose action-invariant kernels \(K:C\rightsquigarrow D\) and \(Q:D\rightsquigarrow Y\) satisfy

\[
P_a^D=P_a^C K,\qquad P_a^Y=P_a^D Q.
\]

Define

\[
\zeta_D=\zeta_CK,\qquad v_D=Qu,\qquad v_C=Kv_D.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\langle\zeta_C,v_C\rangle
=
\langle\zeta_D,v_D\rangle
=
\mathbb E[u(Y)\mid do(A=a)]
-
\mathbb E[u(Y)\mid do(A=a_0)].
}
\]

**Proof.**

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle\zeta_D,v_D\rangle
&=\int_Dv_D(d)\int_CK(dd\mid c)\,\zeta_C(dc)\\
&=\int_C\left[\int_Dv_D(d)K(dd\mid c)\right]\zeta_C(dc)\\
&=\langle\zeta_C,Kv_D\rangle\\
&=\langle\zeta_C,v_C\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]

The downstream factorization identifies this value with the intervention contrast. ∎

This avoids computational gradient decay, but cannot prevent a genuinely mixing environment from erasing the causal effect itself.

**Theorem 2 — Greatest-safe local revision**

**Status: PROVEN on paper; mechanical verification UNKNOWN.**

For every \(X':B\to C\),

\[
\boxed{
P;X';S\subseteq G
\iff
X'\subseteq P\backslash(G/S).
}
\]

Consequently,

\[
R^{\mathrm{safe}}
=
R\cap\bigl(P\backslash(G/S)\bigr)
\]

is the unique greatest safe subrelation of \(R\).

**Proof.** Relational composition obeys

\[
P;X'\subseteq B'
\iff X'\subseteq P\backslash B'
\]

and

\[
X';S\subseteq G
\iff X'\subseteq G/S.
\]

Thus

\[
P;X';S\subseteq G
\iff P;X'\subseteq G/S
\iff X'\subseteq P\backslash(G/S).
\]

Intersecting this greatest admissible relation with \(R\) gives the greatest safe subrelation of \(R\). ∎

Additional results:

- **Substitution invariance:** same typed boundary relation and interventional kernels imply identical external ledger values.
- **Approximation bound:**

\[
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{\mathrm{out}},P'_{\mathrm{out}})
\leq\min(1,n\varepsilon),
\]

with two-arm utility-credit error at most

\[
2U\min(1,n\varepsilon).
\]

- **Finite monotone refinement:** under stationary, noiseless, truth-preserving constraints, at most

\[
\sum_i |R_i^{(0)}|
\]

strict tuple deletions can occur.

- **Conditional active-identification bound:** if each query leaves at most a \(\rho<1\) fraction of the current version space,

\[
N_{\text{queries}}
\leq
\left\lceil\frac{\log N}{\log(1/\rho)}\right\rceil.
\]

The weakest assumptions are interventional boundary sufficiency, correct positive evidence, single-module failure, acyclicity, and bounded interface width.

### E. Mechanism/Architecture Instantiation

```text
BCDL-CYCLE(contracts, contrast a:a0, utility u, verified evidence G)

    update the balanced typed-contract cache

    choose an interventionally sufficient boundary C
    ζC ← P(C | do(a)) - P(C | do(a0))
    vC ← pull u backward through the downstream contract
    store causal entry (ζC, vC, <ζC,vC>)

    if no verified model contradiction exists:
        do not revise the dynamics model
        return causal ledger

    diagnoses ← ∅
    for each candidate module i:
        Pi ← cached prefix
        Si ← cached suffix
        Ai ← Pi \ (G / Si)
        Di ← Ri \ Ai

        if Ei ∩ Di = ∅:
            diagnoses ← diagnoses ∪
              {(i, Di, proof Pi;(Ri∩Ai);Si ⊆ G)}

    if exactly one diagnosis is identified:
        meet its version space with Ai
        recompute only affected cache ancestors
    else:
        request the intervention that best separates diagnoses
        leave the model unrevised
```

For horizon \(H\) and dense boundary carrier size \(q\):

| Operation | Time | Memory |
|---|---:|---:|
| Initial balanced cache | \(O(Hq^3)\) | \(O(Hq^2)\) |
| One local replacement | \(O(q^3\log H)\) | \(O(Hq^2)\) |
| All module residuals | \(O(Hq^3)\) | \(O(Hq^2)\) |
| Kernel credit transport | \(O(Hq^2)\) | \(O(q)\) streaming |

For factor-graph width \(w\),

\[
T=O(Nq^{w+1}),\qquad M=O(Nq^w).
\]

Therefore only cache-update depth is logarithmic. General inference still suffers interface-width and treewidth explosion.

BCDL is operationally distinct from attention, BPTT, and Bellman/reward redistribution. This is not a claim of computational non-simulability: transformers or augmented model-based RL systems could simulate finite BCDL instances.

### F. Empirical Falsification Plan

**MEASURED:** A deterministic 4,000-trial NumPy experiment used horizons

\[
H\in\{4,8,16,32,64\},
\]

positive-evidence coverage

\[
p\in\{0,.25,.5,.75,1\},
\]

boundary size \(q=6\), and one corrupted module per chain.

Results:

- At \(p=0\), unique localization was 0%; every module remained a candidate.
- At \(H=64,p=.75\), unique localization was \(29/160=18.1\%\), exact 95% CI \([12.5\%,25.0\%]\), with 3.525 candidates on average.
- At \(p=1\), localization was \(160/160\) for every horizon.
- The true faulty module remained in the candidate set in every trial.

This **disconfirms** the claim that residuation alone solves long-horizon identification. It computes exact safe repair alternatives, but does not determine which alternative is true.

### G. Comparison to Existing Methods

The novelty audit used arXiv plus Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex discovery. Google Scholar was access-blocked, and Papers With Code produced no useful mapping. Absence from these searches is not evidence of novelty.

| Method | Credit object | Update semantics | Relation to BCDL |
|---|---|---|---|
| BPTT / [synthetic gradients](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05343) | Parameter derivative | Gradient update | BCDL has no parameter derivative or temporal reverse pass. |
| [RUDDER](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07857) | Redistributed return | RL update | BCDL does not redistribute reward. |
| [Counterfactual Shapley](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16999) | Coalition allocation | Policy/reward update | Direct collision with rejected Candidate 1. |
| [Reiter diagnosis](https://cse.sc.edu/~mgv/csce580f11/gradPres/reiter-diagnosis.pdf) | Conflict sets | Consistency repair | Closest precursor to BCDL’s revision ledger. |
| [Dijkstra predicate transformers](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/360933.360975) | Weakest precondition | Logical refinement | Residuation itself is not novel. |
| [Open causal models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07638) | Interventional kernel | Causal composition | Typed causal boundaries are prior art. |
| [Predictive-state representations](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2001/hash/1e4d36177d71bbb3558e43af9577d70e-Abstract.html) | Action-conditioned predictions | State update | Learned BCDL boundaries may reduce to PSR sufficiency. |
| **BCDL** | Signed intervention measure plus certified deletion | Identified version-space meet | Proposed integration; novelty remains **UNKNOWN**. |

The proposed novelty delta is only this invariant:

> Instrumental action credit and epistemic model correction must inhabit separate algebraic ledgers, even though both operate over the same typed causal contracts.

The novelty claim is falsified if the complete mechanism reduces routinely to standard diagnosis plus open-causal composition or PSR sufficiency.

### H. Failure Modes & Boundary Conditions

Strongest objections:

1. **Boundary discovery is unsolved.** A compact interventionally sufficient boundary may be as difficult to learn as the full world model.
2. **Diagnosis is not identification.** The experiment showed rapidly increasing ambiguity with horizon.
3. **Novelty may collapse to established mathematics.** Both ledgers have close individual precursors.
4. **No absolute non-equivalence claim is defensible.** Universal architectures can simulate the mechanism.
5. **Monotone deletion is brittle under noise and nonstationarity.**
6. **Interacting actions lack a unique fair allocation.**
7. **Interface-width explosion can dominate balanced depth.**
8. **True causal influence can physically decay.**
9. **Feedback systems require an equilibrium or trace semantics.**

Adversarial cases include hidden bypass paths, observationally equivalent but interventionally different worlds, aliased boundary states, \(k\)-way fault synergy, unmodeled resource channels, and false verified constraints.

Reject BCDL as a useful direction if:

- sufficient boundary size grows essentially linearly with horizon;
- active interventions do not reduce diagnosis sets faster than unstructured search;
- typed contract reuse fails under domain shift;
- dual-ledger separation gives no repair or retention advantage;
- a formal reduction shows the full mechanism is merely renamed diagnosis and open-causal composition.

### I. Iteration Step

The weakest assumption is the existence of a fixed, compact, interventionally sufficient boundary with enough positive evidence to prevent false repair.

The next variant is **Query-Preserving Boundary Refinement**:

- When a required deletion intersects protected positive evidence, interpret the conflict as boundary aliasing.
- Find the cheapest predicate separating the positive and negative witnesses.
- Split the boundary state.
- Recompute both ledgers.
- Use interventions to distinguish remaining diagnoses.
- Replace irreversible meets with provenance-weighted reversible revision under noise.

Next theorem targets:

1. Soundness of witness-separating splits.
2. Finite termination when a sufficient finite predicate partition exists.
3. Approximate partition minimality under an MDL objective.
4. Active-query contraction guarantees.
5. Reversible revision without forgetting unaffected contracts.

Evidence ledger:

- **PROVEN:** Two paper-level algebraic theorems under explicit assumptions.
- **MEASURED:** Exact finite checks passed; the numerical study exposed severe identifiability failure.
- **UNKNOWN:** Lean/Coq verification, integrated novelty, and compact-boundary learnability.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** Dual ledgers improve compositional transfer and continual correction.
- **SPECULATIVE:** Boundary refinement could become a general abstraction mechanism.
- **NOT ESTABLISHED:** AGI, human-level reasoning, or superiority over existing systems.
